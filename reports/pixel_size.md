## Post hoc: vessel widths (added after B failed; not pre-registered)

After the catheter check failed, one frame was looked at (frame 000: the proximal right coronary artery about 11 pixels wide, 2.3 mm at A). The experts' masks of two proximal segments are measured as the catheter was, against their normal diameters (Dodge et al., Circulation 1992). Factor = normal ÷ width at A: about 1 if A is the image's scale, about 2 if the images were halved after the spacing was recorded.

| Segment | Frames | Width, pixels | Width at A, mm | Normal, mm | Factor (median, IQR) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 05 (left main) | 377 | 11.0 | 2.28 | 4.5 | 1.97 (1.54–3.36) |
| 06 (proximal LAD) | 384 | 8.5 | 1.71 | 3.7 | 2.16 (1.70–2.85) |
| Catheter (6 F) | 644 | 5.3 | 1.11 | 2.0 | 1.80 (1.56–1.98) |

Normal diameters are population means (men and women differ; diseased segments are narrower), so the factor is approximate. The paper (arXiv:2607.22139) gives neither the original image size nor the grid the spacing refers to.

**Limits:** the isocentre is not the vessel's depth (P5: about 4% median error at the vessel). The catheter's French size is not recorded (6 F assumed; 5–7 F span ± 17%), and the catheter sits near the aortic root. The width rule (2·d − 1 on the skeleton) reads even widths one pixel narrow. One vendor (Siemens) and one region (northern Poland): the product's default is not changed from this report.
